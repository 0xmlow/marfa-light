Questions

Before you mint.

What am I minting?

One of 111 Marfa Light tokens: a working clock in 3D for Marfa, Texas, drawn live in your browser by a program stored on Ethereum, with three.js from the Art Blocks dependency registry. It is art, not an investment.

Who decides which clock I get?

The token's hash, fixed when your transaction lands. It chooses the clock (29 kinds), where it stands (22 places), its materials, the sky and about 44 traits in all. Nobody chooses it, MLow included.

What does it cost?

0.069 ETH plus gas, one token per transaction. The price on the button is the most the contract will take; if the terms change, the transaction fails and nothing is charged.

Does it change after I mint?

It keeps Marfa time. The sun, the moon and the stars move with the real sky over Marfa, shadows read the hour, and the calendar arrives on its own: fireworks on the Fourth of July, the lights of Hanukkah, the Marfa lights over Labor Day weekend.

What happens when I press mint?

Your wallet signs, the reels spin while the block is mined, and the moment it lands the page reads your token's hash from the chain. The reels stop on your clock, your place and your sky, and the clock draws itself right there, live, in 3D. Save a still, download the 3D model, or post it. Want to see it first? Press Watch a reveal to replay a pull that already happened.

Where can I see it?

Every token opens a live 3D view, linked from the gallery above, from the collectors page and from OpenSea. Its day GIF thumbnail appears within the hour, and the collectors page lets you download the GIF, a still and the 3D model (GLB).

Who made it?

MLow, a New York artist, with written permission from Snowfro, Alexis André and Bryan Brinkman for the Art Blocks works that appear inside the clocks. Read the story.
