The artist

A New Yorker of 27 years.

MLow came to code through models. Since 2018 he has trained on his own paintings and curated by hand, building whole worlds out of one body of work: a census of New York, a conspiracy of operatives, a garden of flowers that watch you back.

His work has ridden on more than 5,000 New York City taxis with Somo and Art Crush Gallery, hung in 50 exhibitions shown in 14 countries, and was part of a campaign that raised $250K for St. Jude. He hosts The MLow Show, conversations with AI and onchain artists, on the Rug Radio network.

He survived cancer. The eye flower that keeps opening in his work, and the blue evil eye he signs with, both come out of that recovery. Marfa Light carries it too: an eye flower that turns with the sun.

The story

How 111 clocks came to keep Marfa time.

  1. 2026

    It started with Memories.

    An open source system MLow built to store a drawing program onchain: 612 directions in 87 families. The sun model inside Marfa Light was born there.

  2. September 2026

    Twenty nine ways to tell the time.

    Eight sundials built for latitude 30.3°, a nocturnal that reads the Big Dipper, and the rest keeping time by flaps, water, sand, words, gears, fire, wind and a Foucault pendulum. Each one stands somewhere real around Marfa.

  3. September 30

    In dialogue with Art Blocks.

    With written permission from Snowfro, Alexis André and Bryan Brinkman, the Chromie Squiggle and Friendship Bracelets run inside the clocks as credited homages.

  4. October 1

    Proposed for four weeks in Marfa.

    The series went to the Art Blocks x OpenSea Artist Residency with a plan: measure the real sky, check the dials against a real gnomon, and lock the series in the light it was written for.

  5. October 1

    The calendar arrives.

    Fireworks on the Fourth of July, a Hebrew calendar computed from the molad, Halloween pumpkins, solstice lines, and on Labor Day weekend the Marfa lights drifting over the horizon.

  6. October 6

    Live on Ethereum.

    Marfa Light became the first generative art project deployed on ABX on Ethereum. The script and three.js live onchain; the sale opened at 0.069 ETH.

  7. October 9

    The lights move to measured bearings.

    On this site, the Marfa lights now appear along the sight lines Zach Warren measured in his study of the phenomenon, credited in the code.

  8. October 22

    Clocks for the people who shaped the work.

    MLow gifted clocks to 54 artists and collectors, from Snowfro to the 6529 Museum. 69 of 111 are now keeping time; 42 are still waiting.
